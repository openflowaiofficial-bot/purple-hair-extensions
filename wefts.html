Method 02

Hand-Tied
& Machine
Wefts

A weft is a woven row. Ours are woven double-drawn — every strand full length from root to tip — so the row you install in month one is still the row she has in month ten.

Two constructions

Hand-tied hides. Machine-tied holds.

Stylists ask which is better. Neither — they solve different problems, and the right answer is usually both in the same head.

Hand-tied seam — 1.2 mm return, lies flush
Construction A

Hand-tied

Each strand knotted onto the return by hand. The seam is thin enough that a client with fine hair can wear a row high on the head and still wear a ponytail. It cannot be cut without unravelling — order it to width.

  • Thinnest seam available, invisible on fine hair
  • Sized to order in 1″ increments
  • Best for high placement and short layers
  • Beaded row or sew-in
Machine weft — cut and sealed mid-row, no shed
Construction B

Machine-tied

Sewn on a machine into a sealed row you can cut anywhere without shedding. Heavier seam, far more density per row, and the flexible choice when you are covering a wide back section.

  • Cuttable — one weft covers several placements
  • Highest density per row
  • Best for thick hair and full back coverage
  • Beaded, sewn, or hybrid installation

The house standard

Four things we will not sell you.

Most complaints in this trade come from the same four shortcuts. We do not take them, and the price reflects that.

  • Single-drawn sold as double. If a bundle tapers at the ends, it is single-drawn. Ours does not taper.
  • Acid-bathed hair. Stripping the cuticle makes hair cheap to process and dead in three washes. Ours keeps its cuticle.
  • Silicone coating. A gloss that washes out and takes the client's trust with it. Ours arrives uncoated.
  • Pre-lightened blondes. We tone to level in-house rather than bleaching on arrival, so the hair still takes color when you need it to.
